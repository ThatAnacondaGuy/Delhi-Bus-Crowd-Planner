# Phase 1 research notes (18 Nov 2026)

Supporting evidence for the Phase 1 design (`2026-11-17-phase1-crowding-planner-design.md`). Gathered by researcher agents from public sources and checked by a second agent where marked. Public facts and aggregate statistics only; no ticket-level or vehicle-level data.

## 1. Busy bus corridors (external evidence only; pre-registered)

Chosen without looking at the ticket data or any model output, so the corridor check in the validation suite is not circular.

### 1. Ambedkar Nagar/Khanpur – Chirag Delhi – Moolchand – Mathura Road – ITO – Delhi Gate – Old Delhi (former BRT Corridor 1) (strong)

- Roads and endpoints: Josip Broz Tito (JBT) Marg from Dr Ambedkar Nagar via Pushp Vihar, Chirag Delhi and Siri Fort to Moolchand (Hidalgo & Pai 2009). It continues north via Defence Colony and Jangpura to Mathura Road, Bhairon Road/Pragati Maidan, ITO and Delhi Gate (2005 design-summary schematic). The radial routes on it (423, 419) run on through Daryaganj to Mori Gate / ISBT Kashmere Gate / Old Delhi.
- Routes mentioned: 423UP, 423DOWN, 419UP, 419DOWN, 534UP, 534DOWN
- Peak direction or time: Morning peak, 8–10 AM. July 2008 counts at Chirag Delhi: about 6,350–6,370 bus passengers per hour (both directions together) from 8–10 AM, against about 2,100–2,900 per hour from 5–8 PM; counts were similar in both directions. Photos in the report illustrating high bus occupancy were taken south-to-north at 8:45–9 AM. AAP (2025) reports waits of 40+ minutes at Chirag Delhi around 9 AM.
- GTFS stops to test: Ambedkar Nagar Terminal; Khanpur Ambedkar Nagar; Pushp Vihar; Chirag Delhi; Siri Fort Road; Mool Chand; Defence Colony; Bhogal (Jungpura); Pragati Maidan (Bhairon Marg); ITO BSZ Marg (Ram Charan Aggarwal Chowk); Delhi Gate; Darya Ganj; Mori Gate Terminal
- Sources:
  - [ITDP, Institutional and Regulatory Options for Delhi's High Capacity Bus System (1 May 2004)](https://www.itdp.in/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Delhi-HCBS-Institutional-Reform.pdf): The first 6.3 km section of Delhi's planned HCBS corridor carried about 135 buses per hour per direction (268 in total), roughly 7,000 passengers per direction in the peak hour. ITDP also notes this section was chosen for ease of engineering rather than for maximum impact.
  - [First Delhi BRT Corridor: A Design Summary, Ambedkar Nagar to Delhi Gate (Dec 2005; TRIPP-IIT Delhi/RITES design process)](https://www.sgarchitects.in/images/research/pt/BRTDesign.pdf): In 2001–02 a committee recommended 14 bus-priority corridors, chosen on available right-of-way and the present level of bus services. Dr Ambedkar Nagar–Masjid Moth–Moolchand–Sundar Nagar–Appu Ghar–Delhi Gate–Lal Qila–ISBT (19 km) was one of five first-phase priority corridors, and Ambedkar Nagar–Delhi Gate was picked as the first BRT corridor.
  - [Hidalgo & Pai (EMBARQ/WRI) for CSE, Delhi Bus Corridor: An Evaluation (Feb 2009)](https://www.cseindia.org/content/downloadreports/52): The corridor runs along JBT Marg from Moolchand to Ambedkar Nagar. At Chirag Delhi junction in the morning peak, buses were about 2% of vehicles but moved about 55% of people. 112 buses per hour carried 6,371 people (57 per bus). The report lists a peak load of 6,500 passengers per hour per direction and flags high peak-hour bus occupancy. The July 2008 survey counted 6,356 bus passengers from 8–9 AM and 6,371 from 9–10 AM.
  - [AAP press release (4 Nov 2025; opposition party, partisan)](https://aamaadmiparty.org/press-release/dtc-buses-in-delhi-in-shambles-people-waiting-for-hours-at-bus-stops-a-common-sight-saurabh-bharadwaj/): Says routes 423 and 419, from Khanpur/Ambedkar Nagar to Mori Gate/Old Delhi, have the most serious problems. Commuters at Chirag Delhi around 9 AM said they had waited 40+ minutes for route 534, and waits for route 419 reach an hour or more.
  - [Millennium Post, AAP hits out at BJP over Delhi's bus blues (5 Nov 2025)](https://www.millenniumpost.in/delhi/aap-hits-out-at-bjp-over-delhis-bus-blues-634113): Reports the same AAP claims: the worst shortages are on routes from Khanpur and Ambedkar Nagar to Mori Gate and Old Delhi, with 40+ minute waits at Chirag Delhi for 534 and 419. This relays the same source and is not independent.
  - [The Quint, Delhi to have dedicated bus lanes from 1 April (24 Mar 2022)](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/delhi-bus-lanes-fine-all-you-need-to-know): Lists ITO–Ambedkar Nagar among the first-phase dedicated bus-lane corridors.
  - [Bhatia & Jain, Bus Transport in Delhi, CCS Working Paper 210 (2009)](https://old.ccs.in/internship_papers/2009/bus-transport-in-delhi-210.pdf): Describes route 534 as one of the busiest and most crowded bus routes, and says the stated frequency is not kept in practice. AAP places route 534 at Chirag Delhi.
  - [India Environment Portal clipping of Times of India, Study junks govt's BRT logic (16 Jul 2012)](https://www.indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/study-junks-govts-brt-logic): Counter-evidence: a CRRI study found buses bunching on the BRT stretch with only 4–5 passengers boarding or alighting per bus, and argued this exaggerated the reported passengers per hour per direction.

### 2. ITO/Mandi House – Vikas Marg – Laxmi Nagar – Preet Vihar – Karkardooma – Anand Vihar ISBT (plus East Delhi links to Geeta Colony, Patparganj, Mayur Vihar) (strong)

- Roads and endpoints: Vikas Marg (Road 57) east from ITO across the Yamuna through Laxmi Nagar, Shakarpur, Nirman Vihar, Swasthya Vihar/Preet Vihar and Karkardooma to Anand Vihar ISBT. The central-Delhi end is Mandi House/ITO. Reports also name the East Delhi branches to Geeta Colony, Patparganj and Mayur Vihar Ph-1.
- Routes mentioned: 611UP, 611DOWN, 740UP, 740DOWN, 85UP, 85DOWN, 73UP, 73DOWN, 534UP, 534DOWN
- Peak direction or time: Evening peak, eastbound from Mandi House/ITO toward Laxmi Nagar and Anand Vihar. AAP (2025) describes evening queues at Mandi House; ETV Bharat (Oct 2025, the pre-Diwali period) describes ITO to Laxmi Nagar.
- GTFS stops to test: Mandi House; ITO AGCR /  ITO; Laxmi Nagar (Vikas Marg) / Laxmi Nagar Metro Station; Laxmi Nagar X-ing; Shakarpur (Vikas Marg); Nirman Vihar; Swasthya Vihar / Preet Vihar; KarKarDooma Crossing; Anand Vihar ISBT Main Road; Geeta Colony; Patparganj / Patparganj More; Mayur Vihar Phase-1 / Mayur Vihar Phase-1 Metro Station
- Sources:
  - [ETV Bharat, Delhi's public transport in disarray as bus shortage worsens ahead of Diwali (16 Oct 2025)](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/delhis-public-transport-in-disarray-as-bus-shortage-worsens-ahead-of-diwali-enn25101601961): Names ITO to Laxmi Nagar, Geeta Colony, Patparganj and Mayur Vihar as major routes with acute bus shortages. A commuter waited an hour at ITO for a Laxmi Nagar bus and said the buses that do come are over capacity. A Nirman Vihar commuter said there was no room to board. Route 611 riders said their 10-minute service had broken down.
  - [AAP press release (4 Nov 2025; partisan)](https://aamaadmiparty.org/press-release/dtc-buses-in-delhi-in-shambles-people-waiting-for-hours-at-bus-stops-a-common-sight-saurabh-bharadwaj/): Describes evening waits of up to an hour at Mandi House, with buses to Laxmi Nagar, Anand Vihar and Chand Cinema scarce. Says buses to Laxmi Nagar are so few that they run overcrowded, and that Anand Vihar routes 85, 740 and 73 have fewer buses.
  - [Millennium Post (5 Nov 2025)](https://www.millenniumpost.in/delhi/aap-hits-out-at-bjp-over-delhis-bus-blues-634113): Relays the AAP claim that buses toward Anand Vihar and Laxmi Nagar run overcrowded. Same claimant, so not independent.
  - [First Delhi BRT Corridor: A Design Summary (Dec 2005)](https://www.sgarchitects.in/images/research/pt/BRTDesign.pdf): Anand Vihar–Karkardooma Chowk–Swasthya Vihar–Lakshmi Nagar–ITO–Bara Khamba Road–Shivaji Terminal (15 km) was one of five first-phase priority bus corridors. They were drawn from corridors chosen partly on the present level of bus services.
  - [Delhi Planning Department, Transport chapter (Annual Plan 2013–14)](https://delhiplanning.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/Planning/generic_multiple_files/59_79_transport.pdf): A BRT corridor from ISBT Anand Vihar to CP via Road 57, Vikas Marg and DDU Marg (11 km) was earmarked (PWD Package IV).
  - [Time Out Delhi, 56 women-only electric bus services are now running (21 Aug 2026)](https://www.timeout.com/delhi/news/56-women-only-electric-bus-services-are-now-running-in-delhi-082126): ITO is among the hotspots linked by DTC's 15 high-demand Ladies Special routes. These run at peak times, about 8–9 AM and 4:30–6:15 PM.

### 3. Ring Road (Mudrika / Teevra Mudrika loop), including TRIPP's Moolchand–LSR College–Nehru Place spur (moderate)

- Roads and endpoints: Inner Ring Road loop: ISBT Kashmere Gate – Rajghat – ITO – Sarai Kale Khan – Maharani Bagh/Ashram – Lajpat Nagar – Moolchand – South Extension – AIIMS – Bhikaji Cama Place – Dhaula Kuan – Naraina – Raja/Rajouri Garden – Punjabi Bagh – Azadpur. TRIPP's 2005 priority corridor ran Azadpur → Dhaula Kuan → South Ext → Moolchand, then via LSR College to Nehru Place. The design summary names that Moolchand arm toward Nehru Place 'Lala Lajpat Rai Path'.
- Routes mentioned: TMS(+)UP, TMS(-)DOWN
- Peak direction or time: No time of day stated. The 2024 DTE/DTC data names Kashmere Gate, Sarai Kale Khan, Maharani Bagh and Lajpat Nagar as the main boarding stops on the TMS 'up' half. Time Out (2026) lists AIIMS and Kashmere Gate among peak-hour high-demand hotspots.
- GTFS stops to test: ISBT Kashmere Gate Ring Road; RajGhat Ring Road; ITO Ring Road; ISBT Sarai Kale Khan; Maharani Bagh; Ashram Chowk; Lajpat Nagar (Ring Road); Mool Chand; Lady Shri Ram College; Kailash Colony; South Extension; AIIMS Ring Road; Netaji Nagar / Bhikaji Cama Place; Dhaula Kuan (Ring Road); Naraina Village (Ring Road); Rajouri Garden Ring Road; Punjabi Bagh Ring Road; Azadpur Terminal
- Sources:
  - [Down To Earth (Caleb Philip, CSE, and Shilpa Shinde, DTC MD), DTC pilot to redesign bus route improves services, adds passengers (8 Aug 2024)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/dtc-pilots-one-bus-route-design-to-demonstrate-improved-services-and-passengers): TMS is a 51 km loop on the inner Ring Road from Kashmere Gate ISBT. From 1 Apr 2023, frequency between Kashmere Gate and Dhaula Kuan was doubled (every 20 min to every 10 min). Ridership then rose 8% (about 36,000 more commuters) in two months, and trips per day went from 168 to 248. Main boarding stops: Kashmere Gate, Sarai Kale Khan, Maharani Bagh, Lajpat Nagar.
  - [First Delhi BRT Corridor: A Design Summary (Dec 2005)](https://www.sgarchitects.in/images/research/pt/BRTDesign.pdf): Azadpur–Wazirpur Industrial Area–Punjabi Bagh–Raja Garden–Naraina Vihar–Dhaula Kuan–Moti Bagh–South Extn–Mool Chand–LSRC–Nehru Place (32 km) was one of five first-phase priority bus corridors. At the Moolchand junction the arm toward Nehru Place is named Lala Lajpat Rai Path.
  - [The Quint (24 Mar 2022)](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/delhi-bus-lanes-fine-all-you-need-to-know): Lists Britannia Chowk to Dhaula Kuan (western Ring Road) among the first-phase bus-lane corridors.
  - [Wikipedia, Mudrika Seva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudrika_Seva): Mudrika runs on the 55 km inner Ring Road with buses in both directions about every 10 minutes. Teevra Mudrika is described as a lifeline for many commuters because of its high frequency.
  - [Time Out Delhi (21 Aug 2026)](https://www.timeout.com/delhi/news/56-women-only-electric-bus-services-are-now-running-in-delhi-082126): AIIMS and Kashmere Gate are among the hotspots on the 15 high-demand Ladies Special routes, which run at peak hours.

### 4. Mehrauli–Badarpur (MB) Road: Saket – Khanpur – Sangam Vihar/Hamdard Nagar – Tughlakabad – Badarpur (moderate)

- Roads and endpoints: MB Road from Mehrauli/Lado Sarai via Saket, Khanpur (Devli Road crossing), Batra Hospital, Hamdard Nagar/Sangam Vihar, Tughlakabad Fort and Okhla More to Pul Prahladpur and Badarpur. The 2022 bus-lane stretch runs from Anuvrat Marg T-point to Pul Prahladpur T-point.
- Routes mentioned: none
- Peak direction or time: Morning. In the PTI report of 30 Dec 2021, commuters blocked MB Road in the morning when capacity-capped buses would not let them board. No direction or regular peak is stated otherwise.
- GTFS stops to test: Lado Sarai Crossing; Saket Crossing; Khanpur Extension / Devli Road Xing  / Dakshin Puri G-Block; Batra Hospital (Satya Narayan Mandir); Hamdard Nagar / Sangam Vihar; Tuglakabad Fort; MB Road / Okhla More; Pul Pehladpur (Badarpur); Badarpur M B Road / Rajiv Gandhi Stadium
- Sources:
  - [Free Press Journal (PTI), People block MB Road, damage DTC buses for not being allowed to board (30 Dec 2021)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/delhi-people-block-mb-road-damage-dtc-buses-for-not-being-allowed-to-board-amid-covid-restrictions): Commuters blocked MB Road (case registered at Sangam Vihar police station) after DTC buses capped at 17 passengers under Covid rules would not take them. Police put the incident at around 10:30 AM. This shows heavy morning demand at the Sangam Vihar stretch.
  - [The Quint (24 Mar 2022)](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/delhi-bus-lanes-fine-all-you-need-to-know): Lists the MB Road stretch from Anuvrat Marg T-point to Pul Prahladpur T-point among the first-phase bus-lane corridors.
  - [Delhi Planning Department, Transport chapter (2013–14)](https://delhiplanning.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/Planning/generic_multiple_files/59_79_transport.pdf): A DIMTS BRT corridor 'Badarpur to Airport (MB Road, Mehrauli–Mahipalpur Road)', 25 km, was proposed.
  - [AAP press release (4 Nov 2025; partisan)](https://aamaadmiparty.org/press-release/dtc-buses-in-delhi-in-shambles-people-waiting-for-hours-at-bus-stops-a-common-sight-saurabh-bharadwaj/): Says the worst problems are on routes 423 and 419 from Khanpur and Ambedkar Nagar toward Mori Gate and Old Delhi.
  - [Down To Earth (CSE), To catch a Delhi bus (18 Mar 2019)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/to-catch-a-delhi-bus-63619): Calls MB Road one of the most congested roads in Delhi. This is about traffic congestion, not bus loads.

### Recognising 9 m routes

1) Route name. DEVI (9 m) routes carry a D- number. ETV Bharat (May 2025) said each DEVI route has a special number such as D-001. Moovit's DTC DEVI list (29 Jan 2026) shows 9 m routes D-8701 to D-9917. ThePrint (May 2026) cites D-7701. A route_short_name match on ^D-\d (direction suffix stripped) gives 302 GTFS rows: DTC 204, DIMTS 98; 46 are STL depot shuttles. These are 142 distinct numbers: DTC 105 (13 three-digit, 92 four-digit), DIMTS 40 (33 three-digit, 7 four-digit); D-002, D-004 and D-074 are used by both. D- routes carry 12,650 of 80,357 GTFS trips (15.7%). In tickets: 291 of 2,259 route strings, 161,921 of 2,559,899 tickets (6.3%), 139 numbers. D-026B and D-9909 appear in tickets but not GTFS; 5 GTFS D- numbers have no tickets. Moovit's list matches 23 of 24 numbers in GTFS. ETV's 2025 three-digit list matches only 3 of 10, so the numbering was revised. No other name works: FDR, feeder, mohalla, devi and mini match 0 GTFS rows. The 16 DMRC-feeder-style MC/ML rows are served by mixed registration blocks, so they do not mark 9 m buses. 2) Vehicles. 1,509 vehicles have 5 or more D- tickets in October 2026 (1,801 have at least one). That is close to the 9 m fleet: 1,588 in March 2026 plus 307 added in Jul-Aug 2026. Series [registration series omitted](1,214 vehicles, likely 12 m) is almost never on D- routes: 67 of its 275,794 tickets. Of vehicle-linked D- tickets, 74% come from [registration series omitted]-8399 and 22% from [registration series omitted]-9999. 3) The D- rule misses much of the 9 m service. D-touching vehicles take 62% of their own tickets on other routes. 175 non-D route strings (170 GTFS rows, 142 of them DTC; 7.2% of all tickets) get at least 50% of their vehicle-linked tickets from D-touching vehicles. At 90% or more it is 90 strings (86 GTFS rows; 2.0% of tickets), e.g. 456, 847, 848, 840, 907, 911, 307A, 953, 378, 408, 793. This fits ThePrint ('More often than not, DEVi buses run on the same routes as the existing blue buses') and trucksbuses (about 50% of DEVI buses may move to big-bus routes).

Patterns:
- ^D-\d with direction suffix (UP/DOWN/(+)/(-)) stripped. DEVI numbering, both agencies. Examples: D-0002STLUP, D-014DOWN, D-5503UP, D-8702DOWN, D-9101UP.
- Four-digit D-xxxx: mostly DTC, the 2025-26 renumbered DEVI routes (e.g. D-8701..D-8803, D-9101, D-9601, D-9901..D-9917 per Moovit).
- Three-digit D-0xx: mostly DIMTS/DoT DEVI routes (e.g. D-008, D-014, D-018, D-026, D-060, D-074, D-125).
- Vehicle rule (inference): a non-D route is likely 9 m-operated if at least 50% of its vehicle-linked tickets come from vehicles with 5 or more D- tickets. 175 strings / 170 GTFS rows at 50%; 90 strings / 86 GTFS rows at 90%, e.g. 456, 847, 848, 840, 907, 911, 307A, 953, 378, 408, 793.
- Not 9 m markers: FDR, feeder, mohalla, devi, mini (0 rows); MC127/MC137/MC715/ML06/ML-96 DMRC-feeder routes (mixed fleet).

Confidence: High that D- routes are the DEVI routes. Medium that they are run almost only by 9 m buses: the count of D-touching vehicles matches the 9 m fleet and the likely-12 m [registration series omitted]series is absent, but some D- routes get up to about 58% of their tickets from vehicles that rarely run D- routes. Medium-low for the vehicle rule that flags 9 m buses on ordinary routes: it rests on an inference, as no public registration-to-size list was found. Registration series alone do not separate 9 m from 12 m.

## 5. GPS stop-time test (15 Oct 2026, 24 route-directions)

Feasible as ground truth: no. Effort to build as a check: about 1 day.

**Method.** Data and read-only access: I opened the 15 Oct 2026 day file with sqlite3 using file:...?mode=ro&immutable=1. One full scan took 9.4 s and copied 1,638,918 rows for 24 route-directions into a scratchpad parquet. That is 1,013,355 unique (vehicle, timestamp) pairs; about 38% of the rows are duplicate polls. The 24 route-directions are both directions of 12 busy routes: 8 DTC routes (717A, 511A, 729, 623, 522, 433, 578, 764) and 4 cluster routes (774, 539, 706, 727). The main .db file is unchanged (size and modified time are the same). Two things differ from the brief: the file header is in WAL mode, and the database already has two indexes (vehicle_id and timestamp). Tickets come from data/processed_/resolved_tickets.parquet. In it, vehicle_number is a real NULL (missing) for 41% of rows, not the literal string 'NULL'. Stops come from data/processed_/canonical_route_stops.parquet, and shapes from GTFS shapes.txt.  How the method changed: I tested the method you specified first: stationary spells (moving under 0.75 m/s between pings) within 30 m of each stop. It finds a stop on only 15-18% of passes, because with pings about every 11 s a short stop usually falls between two pings. The `speed` column is unusable: stationary pings often report 10-25 km/h and moving pings 2-5 km/h. So I measured zone traversal time instead. Each ping is projected onto the route's GTFS shape (95% of stops lie within 11.5 m of it, and the along-route position is kept from going backwards). For each bus pass, the interpolated time to cross the stretch from 30 m before to 30 m after the stop is T; I repeated everything with 50 m as a check. Rules for a usable pass: - the two pings on either side of each zone edge are no more than 35 s apart; - T is 180 s or less, and no single stationary spell is over 3 minutes (only 127 passes dropped for this); - the stop is not among the first two or last two of the route, has no depot or terminal in its name (60 stops), and lies within 50 m of the shape.  The result is 1,196 stops in scope, 83,022 passes, of which 55,026 (66%) are usable. As a congestion control I also timed the same 60 m stretch halfway between stops (mid-block windows), averaged per route and hour, and also just before and after each stop.  App activity per stop and hour: boardings are counted at the boarding stop in the hour of purchase. Alightings are placed at purchase time plus the GTFS template time multiplied by a GPS-measured pace factor for each route and hour. Two versions: the average over clean weekdays (per the team's calendar: 2 and 20 Oct dropped, plus partial exclusions on 6, 19 and 29 Oct), and 15 Oct alone. Activity is divided by the number of bus passes that hour.  Tests run: - Spearman correlations: pooled, within each route, within each stop across hours, and across stops. - A null test: mid-block time (no stop) against app activity. - A noise ceiling from split-halves (random halves of passes within each cell; odd versus even clean days). - A partial correlation controlling for congestion. - A weighted slope with a bootstrap over stops. - A per-pass test: app tickets with a vehicle number on 15 Oct matched to that exact bus pass at the boarding and alighting stops, compared within the same stop and hour. - The response of T to the gap since the previous bus. - Signal-like mid-block windows: at least 25% of passes held more than 30 s. - Route effects: a fixed effect per route, slope per route, DTC against cluster. - Terminal effect: a model fitted on mid-route stops, then residuals at the last and first intermediate stops, with and without the local congestion control.

**Results.** 1. A real signal, but weak to moderate. Per stop and hour, 6-22 h, 8,729 cells with at least 3 usable passes: - Mean T against app boardings plus alightings per bus (clean-weekday average): Spearman 0.36 (0.38 with the 50 m zone). With 15 Oct app counts alone it is 0.27, so single-day app counts are noisier. - Mid-block time against the same app activity (null test): 0.09. - Partial correlation controlling for congestion (route-hour mid-block time and hour): 0.35. - Within routes: median 0.36. All 24 route-directions are positive, from 0.12 (539UP) to 0.53 (522UP). - Across stops (daily means, 1,069 stops): 0.44 (0.45 at 50 m). Within each route across its stops the median is 0.45, and all 24 are positive. - By decile of app activity per bus, mean T rises from 12.4 s to 25.1 s, while mid-block T stays at 7.3-7.5 s. - Noise ceiling: dwell split-half reliability is 0.88 and app reliability 0.94, so the ceiling is about 0.91. Corrected for noise the correlation is still only 0.42 per stop-hour and 0.45 per stop. The weakness is not sampling noise, so more GPS days would not turn dwell into ground truth.  2. Time of day at a single stop is weak. Within a stop across hours: median 0.23 (71% of 575 stops positive). With adjacent mid-block delay controlled: 0.21 (70% positive, sign test z = 9.1). A stop's T moves with the neighbouring mid-block delay about as much (median 0.24) as with app activity. The peak-against-midday comparison fails at single stops: - App data say the morning peak (08-10 h) is busier per bus than midday (12-15 h) at 84% of 682 stops; dwell agrees on that direction at only 49% (55% with local control). - Evening peak (17-20 h): 75% against 52-55%.  Pooled over all stops, the hourly profile of locally controlled excess dwell does follow app activity (Spearman over hours 0.74-0.76; raw T only 0.37-0.39).  3. Size of the effect: dwell cannot set the scale. - Cross-sectional slope: 7.7 s of zone time per app passenger per bus (95% CI 5.4-10.2), or 10.7 s at 50 m (CI 7.3-14.4), with an intercept of about 8 s. - Per-pass test on 15 Oct: 7,303 vehicle-linked tickets; 85.7% matched to the bus pass at the boarding stop and 5,155 at the alighting stop. Within the same stop and hour, passes carrying at least one linked app rider spend only 2.0 s longer in the zone (CI 1.5-2.6), or 1.4 s per app rider. - Gap since the previous bus: only +0.53 s per extra 10 minutes (0.73 s at the busiest quarter of stops). - What a proportional dwell model would predict: published per-passenger times of 1.77-5.35 s (Fletcher & El-Geneidy 2013, Table 3: https://tram.mcgill.ca/Research/Publications/Translink_Dwell_Analysis.pdf) times the team's scale of about 35-79 riders per app rider (spec) gives 60-420 s per app passenger per bus. The observed 8-11 s is one to two orders of magnitude less. - Dwell flattens out as passengers increase. This matches the nonlinear 'economies of scale' effect reported by Glick & Figliozzi 2017 (https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/6803).  4. Route ratios cannot flag routes with unusual app use. - The route effect (dwell not explained by app activity) ranges from 5.4 s (706UP) to 11.7 s (433UP). Seconds of excess dwell per app passenger range from 23 (717ADOWN) to 66 (764UP). - The route effect is partly repeatable: Spearman 0.62 between the two directions of the same 12 routes. - It fails the one check where we know the answer. The team's ridership scaling puts cluster routes at about 2.2 times more riders per app rider (E about 79 against 35-37). Yet cluster routes show no more dwell per app passenger: route effect 7.6 s for cluster against 8.5 s for DTC, median slope 9.7 against 9.7. - Single-route slope confidence intervals span roughly 3-10 times, for example 727UP 16.6 s (9.4-34.4). - The route effect follows app activity per bus (0.40) and congestion (0.25) more than app share.  5. Signals against stops. - Zone time at stops: median 11.8 s, mean 17.0 s, 13% of passes over 30 s. Mid-block windows: median 7.2 s, mean 11.7 s, 6% over 30 s. - 83 of 1,129 mid-block windows behave like signals or bottlenecks. Their mean hold is 15-49 s and follows traffic: highest at 18 h (48.7 s) and high through midday. Their hourly correlation with app activity is 0.18, against 0.50 for stops. - Stops next to such windows have 4-5 s more zone time. At 30 m they also show a weaker correlation (0.30 against 0.37); at 50 m there is no difference. - With pings about 11 s apart and no list of signal locations, waiting at a red light at a stop just before a junction cannot be told apart from passenger dwell. This is the bias Glick & Figliozzi warn about. Chennai GPS buses also showed delay concentrated at a few critical intersections (Khadhir et al. 2020: https://worldtransitresearch.info/research/8277).  6. Terminal effect: no evidence that the last intermediate stops have less dwell than ticket destinations imply. - On these 24 routes, 16.3% of app tickets end at the last stop, against 6.7% starting at the first. - Model fitted on mid-route stops: about 9.7 s per app boarding per bus and 5.9 s per app alighting. - At the last two intermediate stops (89% of app activity there is alighting) the leftover is +2.7 s (CI 1.1-4.5), or +3.9 s at 50 m. So if anything there is more dwell, which would fit riders ticketed to the terminal getting off earlier. - But mid-block delay is also higher near both route ends (mean 12.9-13.9 s against 11.5 s mid-route). Controlling for local congestion, the leftover is +1.3 s (CI -1.3 to 4.4), or +2.7 s (-0.7 to 6.2) at 50 m, and the first stops show about 0. Inconclusive.  7. Side findings worth keeping: - App purchase happens a median of 0.74 minutes after the bus passes the boarding stop. 70.5% fall within 0-3 minutes after, 23.7% before the bus arrives, and 1.3% more than 10 minutes after. This supports using purchase time as boarding time. - GPS pace factors by hour (0.73 at 06 h, 0.99 at 08 h, 1.20 at 12 h, 1.42 at 18 h, 0.79 at 22 h) match the spec's ticket-pair clock for DTC (0.73, 0.95, 1.15, 1.43, 0.81). - 15 Oct (Navratri) had 13,626 app boardings on these routes, against 13,152 on an average clean weekday. - Unrelated readers can still touch the -shm file: its modified time changed at 12:09:30 on 18 Nov, after my only database access (finished at about 12:06; the later scripts read parquet only). I could not verify the TCQSM per-passenger values directly (the TRB server refused or reset the connection), so none are quoted.

**Pitfalls.**
- Ping interval is about 11 s (646 of 717 buses), but 56 buses report every 60 s. Because longer gaps cover more road, about 34% of passes have a gap of more than 35 s straddling a zone edge and cannot be timed. This tilts the sample toward buses that report every 11 s.
- The stationary-spell method as specified detects a stop on only 15-18% of passes, because short stops fall between pings. Use interpolated zone traversal time instead.
- The GPS speed field is unreliable: it is inverted or lagged against position change, so never use it to detect stopping. About 38% of rows are duplicate (vehicle, timestamp) polls; remove them first.
- Dwell cannot be converted to passengers without calibration counts. The response flattens with more passengers (Glick & Figliozzi 2017: https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/6803), and the observed slope is far below what proportional models predict. Even the Bayesian dwell-to-demand method of Sun, Schmoecker & Fukuda 2021 needed boarding counts from a few runs to get boarding and OD flows acceptable (https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/8665).
- Dwell measures boardings plus alightings at stops, not how many people are on board. It cannot tell boarding from alighting, so it cannot check people-on-board levels, which is what the planned field count was for.
- Signals, junction queues and midday congestion inflate dwell independently of passengers. Stop-level dwell is biased at stops near intersections (Glick & Figliozzi 2017), and data from automatic counters tend to overstate the passenger part of dwell (Grise & El-Geneidy 2017: https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/6811). The data hold no list of signal locations; OSM highway=traffic_signals nodes could add one (https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:highway%3Dtraffic_signals), but nothing was downloaded.
- Riders who are not in the app (women riding free, pass holders, cash) still add dwell, so dwell-to-app ratios mix app share with bus type, door use and route geometry. Route-level app-use flags built on them fail the DTC-against-cluster check.
- End-of-route stops sit in more congested areas. Terminal tests need a local congestion control, otherwise they show a spurious excess.
- Only one GPS day (15 Oct, during Navratri) and weekday hours 6-22 h were tested. More days improve reliability, but reliability is not what limits the result (ceiling about 0.91).
- Linear referencing on the shape needs care on ring routes (OMS) and routes whose shape overlaps itself; keep a filter that stops the along-route position going backwards.
- Ticket stop matching and alighting times come from the old processed_ tables and the GTFS 18 km/h template corrected by a pace factor. Re-run on the new pipeline's resolved tickets.
- Opening with mode=ro on this WAL-mode database still writes the -shm file. Use mode=ro&immutable=1 (the -wal file is 0 bytes). Contrary to the brief, the database already has indexes on vehicle_id and timestamp.

**Recommendation.** Do not present GPS dwell as ground truth or as a replacement for the field count, and do not use it to tune E, capacity, the level thresholds, or route-level 'low app use' flags. It does carry an independent passenger signal: across stops Spearman is about 0.44, it is positive on all 24 route-directions, it survives the mid-block null test and the congestion controls, and it shows up per bus pass. But that signal is weak, it is not limited by noise, it says nothing about time of day at a single stop, and it gives no absolute scale. The note should say plainly that no field count was possible.  If the GPS layer makes the 20 Nov cut-off, spend about one day adding a labelled validation item, 'GPS dwell corroboration (relative activity only)'. It would: - Port 08_linref_visits.py and the metrics from 12, 14, 16 and 17 into the gps module behind --gps-raw. - Commit a small stop-by-hour aggregate. - Report the across-stop Spearman, overall and within route, against the model's boardings plus alightings per bus. Write the expectation into expectations.yaml before running: rho of at least 0.3 overall and above 0 on every route. - Report the mid-block null test and the noise ceiling, the network hourly profile with local congestion control, and the per-pass contrast with linked app tickets. - Report the purchase-timing check, which is nearly free and supports the model's 'purchase time = boarding' assumption. - Report the terminal residual only as an inconclusive diagnostic for the alighting correction.  Keep the field count's validation goal on week-to-week repeatability plus this corroboration, worded as relative evidence. Skip it entirely if the core model is late.
